Introduction

This guide covers every spelling pattern a Glossomathia student needs, from the first Greek letters at A1 through the complex literary forms at C2. Each section is self-contained: find your level, master those rules, then move up.

How to use this guide

  • Work through your level first, then use higher levels as preview reading.
  • The General Rules cheat sheet at the end applies to every level — bookmark it.
  • When you see a rule, say the Greek example aloud. Spelling and sound are deeply linked in Modern Greek.
A note on Modern Greek spelling: Modern Greek uses the monotonic system: one accent mark (the tonos ΄) on the stressed syllable of every word with two or more syllables. The spelling of Greek is largely phonemic — once you know the rules, you can spell almost anything you can say.
Level A1

Foundation

The Greek alphabet, basic sounds, the accent, and the first digraphs

1 σ vs ς — two forms of sigma

The letter sigma has two forms. Use σ everywhere except at the very end of a word, where you use ς.

PositionLetterExample
Inside a wordσσπίτι, ιστορία
End of a wordςκαλημέρας, φίλος
2 The accent (tonos)

Every word with two or more syllables carries exactly one accent on the stressed syllable. Capital letters carry no accent.

Upper-Intermediate

Feminine -η vs -α, masculine -ης endings, neuter -υ exceptions, adverbs in -ως

11 Feminine nouns: -η is default, -α after ε, ι, λ, ν, ρ

Feminine nouns take -η by default. They take -α when the preceding letter is ε, ι, λ, ν, or ρ.

EndingPreceding letterExample
-ηDefaultη ζώνη, η φωνή
-αAfter ε, ι, λ, ν, ρη θάλασσα (λ), η χώρα (ρ), η γιαγιά (ι)

16 Double consonants

Modern Greek has very few genuine double consonants. They appear in:

  • Compounds where prefix and root meet: συν- + ναύτης = συνναύτης
  • Learned words: θάλασσα, γραμματική, αλλά
17 Tricky homophones at advanced level
PairDifference
αλλά / άλλααλλά = but  |  άλλα = other things (neut. pl.)
αυτός / αυτώςαυτός = he/himself  |  αυτώς = in person
παρά / παρα-preposition vs. prefix — one word vs. compound

The 5 Rules Never to Forget

  1. σ vs ς — ς only at the end of a word; σ everywhere else.
  2. Verbs end in -ω — never -ο in 1st person singular present.
  3. 3rd person is -ει — γράφει, not γράφε or γράφη.
  4. Every word gets one accent — and capital letters get none.
  5. Feminine default is -η; after ε, ι, λ, ν, ρ it is -α — γιαγιά, θάλασσα, χώρα.
